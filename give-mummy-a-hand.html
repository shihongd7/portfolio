Give Mummy
a Hand.

A museum escape adventure designed for first-time VR guests. Play as a mummy's spirit, control a detached hand, and find your way home before security catches you.

UNITYC#META HAND TRACKINGVRGAMEPLAY SYSTEMS
01 / OVERVIEW

Escape the museum. Return home.

Our team was challenged to create a VR experience for naive guests — people with little or no prior VR experience. With a museum exhibition as our theme, we imagined an exhibit that wants to escape the museum and return to its homeland.

The player takes the role of a mummy's spirit, controlling a detached mummy hand. The journey begins in a storage room that doubles as a tutorial, then continues through an exhibition hall into a high-pressure escape sequence.

03 / CORE TECHNICAL CHALLENGE

Making a hand feel like a controller.

The biggest technical challenge was designing a controller system that felt intuitive for new VR players while making the mummy hand's movement look believable. We prototyped two different approaches, each with its own trade-offs.

Approach A — Hand-Shape Recognition

Used a hand-shape recognizer to detect a predefined gesture and trigger forward movement. This approach ties locomotion to recognizable hand poses.

Approach B — Tracked Hand + Fingertip Detectors

Mapped the mummy hand's rig/animation onto the ghost hand tracked by Meta, tuning multiple parameters to achieve more natural motion. We added detectors to all five fingertips to sense ground contact and movement, using those signals to drive locomotion.

Iteration, not a fabricated result. Both systems presented challenges. This case study documents the two designs without claiming that one was definitively better or that the issues were fully resolved.
